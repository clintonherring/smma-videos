# Voice — [Owner] business or person

Built by `business-voice` from samples of the business's **own** writing (nothing a copywriter or an agency wrote for them). Needs `profile.md`. Every hook, spoken line, caption, text scene and share caption is checked against this file before it is handed back.

The test for every line: *would [Owner] (the person who speaks for the business) say this out loud to one customer across the counter, the bench, the table?* If not, it is rewritten until they would.

## Patterns to copy

Habits observed in the samples, each with a quoted example. Habits, not adjectives ("claim, then the reason in the same sentence", not "friendly").

- [Owner] Sentence shape: first or third person? short or long? one point per sentence? Example: "<quote>"
- Verbs they actually use: <built, fixed, ran, show, book …>
- How they handle numbers and names: in the same sentence as the claim? rounded, exact, with a condition? Example: "<quote>"
- How they explain the why: everyday terms, an analogy, context before the claim, the reason after it? Example: "<quote>"
- How they address the reader: you singular, "we" for the people on the job, the owner's "I"? Example: "<quote>"
- Tone: dry, warm, direct, humorous; what they never do in writing (exclamation marks, buzzwords, hedging, superlatives)

## Words and shapes to avoid

- Marketing-speak: <comma-separated words that sound false in their mouth: seamless, premium, passionate, game-changer, solutions, hassle-free …> (keep the generic list unless they actually use a word themselves)
- Their industry's words for their things that they do not use: "<the trade word>" (they say "<their word>")
- Shapes: noun fragments ("<example>"), passive voice ("<example>"), slogans, group address ("you guys", "everyone", "cyclists"), "we" meaning the company as an institution
- Anything else that is not them: <claims they would not make, jokes they would not tell, a register they would switch off>
