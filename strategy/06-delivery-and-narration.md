# Delivery and narration

How a human speaks on camera, how a narrator reads, and what to do when there is no voice at all. Who speaks for this business is settled in `voice.md` ("Who speaks"); the rules here hold for all of them.

## The out-loud test

Before a take, ask: **would I say this sentence to one friend across a cafe table?** If it feels weird out loud, it will feel weird on camera.

| Forced (breaks the rule) | Relaxed (passes) |
|---|---|
| "Hi. I've discovered three really important things that you need to know." | "Hey. I've found three things you need to know." |
| "As business owners, we all struggle with…" | "Every Sunday I did invoices at the kitchen table." |
| "Studies show most teams are inefficient." | "I used to guess chain wear by eye. Then I started measuring. Same bike, two different bills." |
| "Today I'm going to walk you through…" | "Here's what I do when…" |

Delivery matches the signature line: flat, matter-of-fact, one person. Not a channel host, not a presenter. Do two or three reps to camera; if a line would make a friend hatch an escape plan, rewrite it or drop the energy until it lands across a table.

## Connect, don't perfect

- Wrong: "the internet is watching; I must get every word right."
- Right: "one person is across from me; I am connecting with them."

Perfecting kills connection: stiff shoulders, over-careful diction, eyes that look like they are reading. If you catch yourself performing, stop, shake it out, start again as a conversation.

## One feeling per video

Decide before recording: **how should the one person watching feel when this ends?** Examples by type:

| Type | Feeling to leave them with |
|---|---|
| How-to | "I can do this on the next one." Clear, capable |
| Problem and fix | "They have the same problem I have, and a fix I can try." Recognised, curious |
| Invitation | "I could turn up tomorrow." Welcome, not overwhelmed |
| Story | "I know exactly what that morning was like." Moved, not manipulated |
| Origin | "I trust why this exists." Settled |

If you do not know the feeling, the viewer will not either; that confusion is "emotional spaghetti". Pick one feeling, hold it from the first line to the CTA. The brief records it; if it cannot be inferred, `make-video` leaves the line open and asks rather than inventing one.

## Points with paint

Every claim needs something that makes it land: a story, a concrete example, an analogy, or the thing on screen.

| Point alone | Point with paint |
|---|---|
| "Heat pumps are complicated." | "Her thermostat said 21. The flow temperature on the manifold said 38. We fixed the setting, not the pump." |
| "Our turnaround is fast." | "Dropped off at nine. Riding it home at four." |
| "The old way was slow." | "I used to do every receipt on Sunday night. Now I photograph them in the van. Ten minutes a day." |

Do not stack abstract points without a picture or a cafe-table example. When the "paint" is a shot the business does not have, it is a gap (`10-assets-and-gaps.md`): a text scene can carry a number or a quote; it cannot replace the thing in use.

## Warm up before you record

1. **Body:** shake out arms, shoulders, jaw; a few loud nonsense sounds so the first take is not the warm-up.
2. **Mind:** picture the one person and the feeling. Think about the take landing, not about how hard recording is.
3. **Out-loud reps:** say the hook and the signature to camera twice, as if to a friend.
4. Then roll. Log cold takes as practice unless one accidentally lands.

## Narration (when nobody is on camera)

Many businesses have screen recordings, stills and clips but no one who wants to be on camera. Narration is fine, with rules:

- **Narration is still one person to one person.** Same out-loud test; same voice file. A narrator reading marketing copy is a presenter with the camera off.
- **Narrate the flow, not the screen.** Say what the viewer is about to see happen and why it matters; do not read the labels that are already on screen.
- **Short lines, placed on action.** A line lands on the moment it describes (the tap, the result arriving), not before or after.
- **Generated voices** (`npx hyperframes tts`, or any the business supplies) are acceptable for drafts and for explainers; a real voice is better for stories and invitations. Say in the brief which one, and never generate a voice that imitates a real person without their consent.
- **Captions still burn in.** Narration is a layer on top of a video that works on mute.

## Captions only (no voice)

When there is no voice at all, text scenes and captions carry the lines. The reading floor (`07-edit-fundamentals.md`) becomes the pacing; the signature line becomes a text scene in the brand's style; the feeling is carried by the footage and the order of the lines. Rules in `11-text-scenes.md`.

## Characters

A character (mascot, the van with a face, a named junior) speaks in first person, present tense, in the middle of doing something; never explains stakes; never sells; is never reluctant; never uses jargon (the caption carries the real name and number). The character's affect stays constant; the viewer's feeling is carried by captions and human lines.

## Delivery checklist

- [ ] Feeling for the viewer decided and written on the brief
- [ ] Warmed up; the keeper is not the first take of the day
- [ ] Hook and signature pass the out-loud test
- [ ] Connecting, not perfecting; if stiff, stop and reset
- [ ] Every substantial point has paint
- [ ] No presenter openers ("Welcome back", "In this video I will…")
- [ ] Narration describes the flow, lands on action, and does not read the screen
- [ ] Every line passes the voice check in `voice.md`
