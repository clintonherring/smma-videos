# The signature line

The near-constant 3–6 second line that follows the hook. Viewers should know within six seconds whose video this is and whether it is for them, without the channel name. The business's own line and its variants live in `voice.md` ("Signature line"), written by the `business-voice` guide in the owner's own patterns.

## What it does

- **Identity.** Says who is speaking, in one breath.
- **Side.** Puts the speaker beside the viewer, not above them: same work, same problem, same street. This is the credentials beat; in a short nothing else about the speaker is needed.
- **Consistency.** Repeat viewers recognise it. New viewers learn it in two videos.

## How to write one

There is no fixed formula. Pick the shape that is true for this business and write it the way the owner talks (`voice.md` "Patterns to copy"). The shapes that work:

| Shape | What it says | Example | Business |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Peer** | I do what you do | "I run a one-person shop too. This is what I do about the paperwork." | bookkeeper for sole traders |
| **Presence** | I have been where you are | "Nine years self-employed before I built this. The Sundays were the reason." | small software tool |
| **Place** | I am the one on your street | "Northside Cycle Repair, under the railway bridge. I fix bikes. Probably yours." | bike repair |
| **Promise** | this is the one thing I am for | "I fit heat pumps in old houses. Only old houses." | installer |
| **Character** | a named mascot, robot or animal says who it is and what it does | "I'm Pip. I check the bread before it goes out." | bakery with a mascot |

Rules:

- Speaker first, product never. The product is not in the signature line.
- Said to one person. Not "like many of you", not "all of us", not "guys".
- Delivery is flat and matter-of-fact. It is a statement, not a greeting, not a smile-and-wave.
- Shot where the work happens (bench, kitchen, van, screen), not a studio.
- A signature is false if the viewer does not share what it claims. A second viewer gets its own variant: the joiner who says "I fit kitchens for a living" to homeowners says "I'm the fitter your clients ask you about" to the kitchen showroom.
- If the owner already has a line they say to customers, that is the signature. Do not invent one over it.

## Variants

Approved variants exist so the exact line is not awkward against every hook and so repeat viewers do not skip. Each keeps the same shape and the same claim. Write 3–5 per viewer group in `voice.md`, and one **presence variant** for long form, where the opening is framed as the speaker's own past ("I fitted kitchens for twelve years before I started making them."). Follow a presence variant with the problem, not with a peer claim.

Not approved, for any business:

- "Hey guys, [name] here." (names the group, names you, gives the viewer no identity)
- "Welcome back to the channel." (the viewer does not care about the channel yet)
- "As [group], we all know…" (group address)
- A signature that claims a shared identity the viewer does not have.
- A slogan. If it would fit on a van door, it is not the signature.

## The bridge

One sentence after the signature that turns the hook into a promise. Patterns:

- "That cost me [specific]. Here's what I did about it."
- "Here's the number I use, and where it comes from."
- "Here's what it looks like on my screen and on yours at the same time."
- The point (`02-hooks.md`): one plain sentence for what we are actually after.
- The hand-off: "This one is about [the one idea]."

When the video opens on the default (`02-hooks.md` "The default and its cost"), the bridge is usually the point and the hand-off together.

## Characters and second voices

Some businesses speak through a character (a mascot, a robot, an animal, a named employee). The character has its own signature, its own first-person present-tense voice, and never explains the stakes: the stakes go in captions and in lines from humans. The character's signature names the character and its job. Jargon lives in captions, not in the character's mouth. `voice.md` "Who speaks" holds the rules when this applies.

## Long form

Keep the signature; expand the opening:

```
0–10 s   COLD OPEN     the strongest 10 s of footage from the body, no narration or one line
10–13 s  SIGNATURE
13–30 s  WHAT YOU GET  "By the end you'll know X, you'll have seen Y, and you'll know whether this is for you."
30 s +   BODY
```

Or, when the video opens on the default (origin, offer, "for years I did it this way"): presence → problem as my own past in three specifics → the point → turn ("I do it differently now, and here is how") → what you get, shown → optional "why I'm telling you" → hand-off. Name yourself once, lightly, in the first minute of long form only.

## Checklist before recording an intro

- [ ] First frame is footage of the moment (or a designed text scene carrying the hook), not a face on a wall or a title card
- [ ] Hook has a specific and is addressed to one person
- [ ] Signature is the canonical line or an approved variant for *this* viewer, and passes the out-loud test (`business-voice` mode 2)
- [ ] Bridge is one sentence
- [ ] No product name before the body (default openings: not before Me)
- [ ] No "guys", "everyone", "community", "welcome back"
