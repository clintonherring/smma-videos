# The signature line

The near-constant 3–6 second line that follows the hook. Viewers should know within six seconds whose video this is and whether it is for them, without the channel name. The business's own line and its variants live in `voice.md` ("Signature line"), written by the `business-voice` guide.

## What it does

- **Identity.** Says who is speaking, in one breath.
- **Peer.** Says *I do what you do* or *I am in this with you*. This is the credentials beat; in a short nothing else about the speaker is needed.
- **Consistency.** Repeat viewers recognise it. New viewers learn it in two videos.

## How to write one

Three parts, in this order: **who I am** · **what we share** · **"like you"** (or the equivalent that puts the viewer beside you).

| Business | Signature (example) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Volunteer drone pilot who built a search tool | "I'm a drone pilot. I do search and rescue, like you." | peer first; the shared identity; "like you" said to one person |
| Independent bike mechanic | "I fix bikes for a living. Probably yours." | identity; the viewer's own bike is the shared thing |
| Bookkeeping app for sole traders | "I ran a one-person business for nine years. I built this because of the Sundays." | presence; the lived moment the viewer shares |
| Character (a robot, a mascot, a dog) | "I'm Rover. I help search for animals and nests." | names the character; "help" places it inside a team; concrete work |

Rules:

- Peer first, product never. The product is not in the signature line.
- "Like you" is said to one person. Not "like many of you", not "like all of us".
- Delivery is flat and matter-of-fact. It is a statement, not a greeting, not a smile-and-wave.
- Shot where the work happens (bench, field, screen), not a studio.
- The signature line is false if the viewer does not share the identity. A second viewer who does not get its own variant: "I search for nests, like you. I use a drone so I cover more area, faster." (the shared work first; the tool second).

## Variants

Approved variants exist so the exact line is not awkward against every hook and so repeat viewers do not skip. Each keeps the three parts. Write 3–5 per track in `voice.md`, and one **presence variant** for long form, where the opening is framed as the speaker's own past: "I've searched for nests and deer with teams all over the country." Follow a presence variant with the problem, not with "like you".

Not approved, for any business:

- "Hey guys, [name] here." (names the group, names you, gives the viewer no identity)
- "Welcome back to the channel." (the viewer does not care about the channel yet)
- "As [group], we all know…" (group address)
- A signature that claims a peer identity the viewer does not have.

## The bridge

One sentence after the signature that turns the hook into a promise. Patterns:

- "That cost me [specific]. Here's what I did about it."
- "Here's the number I use, and where it comes from."
- "Here's what it looks like on the screen, and on her phone, at the same time."
- The abstract (`02-hooks.md`): one plain sentence for what we are actually after.
- The hand-off: "This one is about [the one idea]."

When the video opens on the room, the bridge is usually the abstract and the hand-off together.

## Characters and second voices

Some businesses speak through a character (a mascot, a robot, an animal, a named employee). The character has its own signature, its own first-person present-tense voice, and never explains the stakes: the stakes go in captions and in lines from humans. The character's signature names the character and its job. Jargon lives in captions, not in the character's mouth. `voice.md` "Who speaks" holds the rules when this applies.

## Long form

Keep the signature; expand the opening:

```
0–10 s   COLD OPEN     the strongest 10 s of footage from the body, no narration or one line
10–13 s  SIGNATURE
13–30 s  WHAT YOU GET  "By the end you'll know X, you'll have seen Y, and you'll know whether this is for you."
30 s +   BODY
```

Or, when the video opens on the room (origin, offer, "for years I did it this way"): presence → problem as my own past in three specifics → abstract → turn ("I work with teams in X now, and we do it differently") → what you get, shown → optional "why I'm telling you" → hand-off. Name yourself once, lightly, in the first minute of long form only.

## Checklist before recording an intro

- [ ] First frame is footage of the moment (or a designed text scene carrying the hook), not a face on a wall or a title card
- [ ] Hook has a specific and is addressed to one person
- [ ] Signature is the canonical line or an approved variant for *this* viewer
- [ ] Bridge is one sentence
- [ ] No product name before the body (room openings: not before Me)
- [ ] No "guys", "everyone", "community", "welcome back"
