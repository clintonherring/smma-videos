# Hooks

A hook is the first one to three seconds. It decides whether the viewer stays for the signature line. The signature line is not the hook; it comes after.

## What a hook has to do

1. **Name a moment the viewer has lived**, a result they want, or a thing they did not know. Not a topic. "Invoicing" is a topic. "You closed the till at nine and you're still doing invoices at eleven" is a moment. The moments come from `audience.md`.
2. **Be visual at the same time.** The first frame is footage of the thing: the screen, the workshop, the product in use, the before. Never a talking head on a plain background, never a title card. If no footage of the moment exists, that is a gap (`10-assets-and-gaps.md`), and the first frame becomes a designed text scene carrying the hook line, not a logo.
3. **Address one person.** "You", or the speaker's "I" with the viewer implied. Never "hey guys", "everyone", "business owners".
4. **Be specific.** A number, a place, a time of day, a real thing on screen.
5. **Set up the signature line.** The hook should make the signature land as *this person actually knows*.

## Hook types

| Type | Shape | Example (each from a different business) |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain you recognise** | the viewer's moment, in one breath | "You closed the till at nine and you're still doing invoices at eleven." (bookkeeping app) |
| **Contrarian** | a thing most people do that you stopped doing | "I stopped quoting by the hour. Jobs got faster." (joiner) |
| **The default and its cost** | how most people do it, what it costs them, then the turn (below) | "Most shops reorder when the shelf is empty. That's two days with nothing to sell. We don't." (wholesaler) |
| **Specific result** | a number with a timeframe | "Forty loaves, one oven, before the first customer at seven." (bakery) |
| **Did you know** | a fact the viewer did not have, with a consequence | "Your chain is a wear item. Leave it and the cassette goes with it." (bike repair) |
| **Unfinished action** | start the thing, promise the result | "Watch what happens to the quote when I change this one number." (then show it) (software) |
| **Question you want answered** | the question the viewer would ask | "How often should you actually service a heat pump?" (installer) |
| **The gap** (character or two-layer videos) | the speaker says something naive; the caption carries the stakes | "I found the warm spot. I like warm spots." · caption: `Leak located, 0.3 bar/h` (a plumber's thermal-camera mascot) |

## The default and its cost

A framing that runs under many hooks and openings. You are talking to one person *about* the people who do it the usual way: they are the default; you and the viewer are the two who do it differently, or will by the end of the video. (The `videos` repo, where this came from, called it "the room".)

| Party | Who | Pronoun |
|---|---|---|
| The default | most people, the way it is usually done; or your own past | "most shops", "they"; "for years I…" |
| Me | the speaker | "I" |
| You | the one person watching | "you"; "we" meaning the two of us |

The shape, short version (10–15 s before the body):

```
Default    most people <do it this way>, in specifics; or "for years I <did it this way>"
Point      what we are actually after, in one plain sentence, no product in it
Me         signature as presence + the thing shown, as proof (product named here, not before)
You        "this one is about <the one idea>"
```

Rules:

- **The viewer is never the default.** "You" is the one leaving it or already out. If the sentence needs the viewer to be part of "most people", rewrite until they are standing next to you looking at it.
- **Describe the default by cost, not accusation.** "Most shops reorder when the shelf is empty; that's two days with nothing to sell" is a cost. "They're doing it wrong" is a charge. Claims about other people pass the facts gate too.
- **The other way keeps its place.** The default is never "people who do X"; it is X done where it no longer fits. Say when it works, then where it stops.
- **The softest default is your own past.** "For years I quoted by phone" names the default without pointing at anyone. Use it whenever "most people" would sound like a charge.
- **The point has no product in it.** One sentence, true whether or not the viewer ever buys anything, in the business's own register (not a slogan). "A quote should take less time than the job." If it needs the product to be true, it is a feature claim.
- **The product is proof, not pitch.** Named at Me, shown on screen, after the point.
- **One default per video.** One default, one cost, one point, one turn.
- Optional, never the default: a **soft exit door** ("If an evening of invoices is fine with you, you don't need this one") offers a choice instead of a dare.

When to use it: comparison and problem-and-fix videos often open this way; how-to videos usually open from the viewer's own moment instead; origin and offer videos open with all of it. The brief keeps one default-and-cost hook and one viewer's-moment hook among the alternates and picks on the day.

## Hook rules

- Start from the viewer's world, not the product. The product appears after the signature line.
- First person for what happened to you ("I lost", "I built", "I stopped"), then "you" in the body.
- A number in a hook is a number you can defend (`facts.md`).
- Do not hook with a feature. Hook with what the lack of it cost. The feature is the payoff.
- Characters and two-layer videos: the spoken hook is naive, the first-frame caption carries the stakes; both on screen in the first three seconds.
- Every hook passes the voice check (`business-voice` mode 2): in their patterns, numbers said the way they say numbers, no slogan.

## The hook bank

Each business keeps a bank in `hooks.md`: lines written for real ideas, tagged with the backlog ID when used. Three options per video, written in the brief; the strongest is posted, the others kept for reposts, platform variants and the next video on the same idea.

Judge a hook by asking: *would the one person I am talking to stop scrolling because this is about them?* If the answer is "it's about the product", rewrite. For a default-and-cost hook, add: *is the viewer standing next to me looking at the default, or are they part of it?*
