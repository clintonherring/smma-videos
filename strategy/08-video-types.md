# Video types

The first thing to settle about any video is what *kind* of video it is. The type decides the default format and length, the body pattern, the CTA, what the brief must answer, **which scenes must be real footage**, what the video degrades to when they are missing, and how it is paced and composed (`build-video/type-profiles.md`). Whether to make it *now* is a separate question (`09-which-videos.md`).

A video is one type. If it wants to be two, it is two videos.

## The types at a glance

| Type | What it is | Format, length | Body pattern | Default CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **How-to** | Teach one thing the viewer does; the product is the tool in hand | short 25–45 s; long if a whole guide | walk the steps / walk the number: state the rule, show it, show the result | guide CTA: "the full guide is at… ask me below" |
| **Comparison** | Two ways of doing one job and what each costs | short 35–55 s | before/after or side by side; name each one's ceiling; land on the one you use and why | guide or product CTA, depending on whether the winner is a method or a feature |
| **Problem and fix** | A moment the viewer has lived, then what closed it | short 40–55 s | pain you recognise → what it cost → the fix, shown → the payoff; one honest limit | product CTA |
| **Reveal** | A new capability or product shown doing its job for the first time | short 30–45 s | entry → key action → result; the thing appears after the signature | product CTA or "show someone who…" |
| **Launch** | The share-first announcement: hook → reveal → 2–3 sharp highlights → punchline | short 15–30 s | text-forward; one real visual anchors it; designed for sharing | share or link CTA |
| **Walkthrough** | One real job start to finish | long 5–15 min; 3–5 shorts planned first | chapters in time order; real numbers from the day | product CTA, then end card to the next video |
| **Origin / why** | Why this exists, from the person who built it | long 4–9 min; cut under 3 min for LinkedIn | presence → for years I… → abstract → turn → what you get → limits → why I'm telling you | "try it on your next … and tell me what breaks" |
| **Story** | One real moment with a person in it, told in time order | short 30–55 s | the setup → the moment → one human line that carries the weight → what it means | share CTA |
| **Explainer** | How one part of it works, for someone who will never use it themselves | short 40–60 s; LinkedIn cut | what you see → what is really happening (captions carry names and numbers) → why it matters to them | "show this to someone who…" / "talk to [owner]" |
| **Invitation** | Ask one person to take part (book, visit, join, bring something) | short 30–45 s | what their first time looks like; what they would do; who they would be with; never price | "come and…" / "your [people] join from their phones" |
| **Offer / pricing explainer** | The one video that explains what you pay and why | long only | the default (what is usually charged for) → the abstract → how we do it → tiers follow need → what each does not include → "both ways cost something" | "pick the one that matches what you do" |

## Each type in full

Each type has a **scene skeleton** (the storyboard template is pre-filled from it), its **asset needs** (scenes that must be real footage for the type to hold), and a **degrade path** (what the gap report says when they are missing). Durations are defaults for a short.

### How-to

- **Skeleton:** hook, the viewer's moment (3 s) · signature (3 s) · the rule or step stated (4 s) · the step done on screen (10–20 s; sequential if there are sub-steps) · the result (4 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** the one rule or step; the number you can defend; what is on screen while you say it.
- **Must be real:** the step being done (screen recording or clip).
- **Degrades to:** a text-led how-to (step cards in brand style over stills) if only stills exist. Still a how-to, weaker; the gap report says so and asks for the recording.

### Comparison

- **Skeleton:** hook, the room (3 s) · signature (3 s) · method A on screen with its ceiling (8–12 s) · method B on screen with its ceiling (8–12 s) · the one you use and why, one line (4 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** the two methods by name; the cost of the old one in a number; the abstract the winner serves; whether the viewer should switch or understand.
- **Must be real:** at least one side on screen; ideally both.
- **Degrades to:** one side real, the other a text scene naming it and its ceiling (B). With neither, it is an explainer, not a comparison; change the type or provide the clip.

### Problem and fix

- **Skeleton:** hook, the moment (3 s) · signature (3 s) · what it cost, a number (4 s) · the fix in use (12–18 s; entry → key action → result) · the payoff (4 s) · one honest limit (3 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** the exact moment (not the topic); what it cost; the abstract; the fix shown, not described; the limit.
- **Must be real:** the fix in use.
- **Degrades to:** without a clip of the fix this is an explainer. The gap report says: keep the type and provide the clip, or change the type.

### Reveal

- **Skeleton:** hook, the before or the question (3 s) · signature (3 s) · entry (3 s) · key action (6–10 s) · result (5 s) · one line on what it means (3 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** what it does, in `facts.md` first; whether it works today (roadmap gate); who tells it.
- **Must be real:** the key action and the result.
- **Degrades to:** stills of the thing with a text scene for the action is a launch, not a reveal. Say so.

### Launch

- **Skeleton:** hook (2–3 s) · reveal: the name and the one visual (2–4 s) · 2–3 highlights, one per card (5–12 s, sequential) · punchline or outro (2–4 s). Signature is optional and short.
- **Brief must settle:** the one visual that is real; the three highlights (grounded); the punchline; the share line.
- **Must be real:** one UI, copy or visual element from the thing itself.
- **Degrades to:** nothing; this is the one type where text scenes are the centrepiece. With no real element at all it is not buildable: provide a screenshot, a photo or a logo.

### Walkthrough

- **Skeleton (long):** cold open from the body (10 s) · signature (3 s) · what you get (15 s) · chapters in time order (each: the moment, the decision, the number) · CTA · end card. **Shorts:** 3–5 planned before the build, each with its own type (usually how-to, story or problem and fix).
- **Brief must settle:** the day or job with permission to film; which shorts fall out of it; the honesty rule (say what the day actually gave).
- **Must be real:** the whole thing. There is no text-led walkthrough.
- **Degrades to:** if the footage covers only part of the job, brief the part as a story or a how-to and leave the walkthrough `on hold` until a full day exists.

### Origin / why

- **Skeleton (long):** presence (5 s) · problem as my own past, three specifics (15 s) · abstract (5 s) · turn (5 s) · what you get, shown (20 s) · why I'm telling you (10 s) · hand-off · chapters: the day that broke it, what I tried, what I built, where it still falls short, what it costs (pointer to the offer video).
- **Brief must settle:** real before-footage or an honest re-enactment; the abstract; the limits chapter.
- **Must be real:** the person, on camera or in voice; something of the before (photos count).
- **Degrades to:** a narrated version over stills and screens is acceptable; a text-only origin is not (change to an explainer).

### Story

- **Skeleton:** hook, the moment mid-action (3 s) · signature (3 s) · the setup, one line (4 s) · the moment unfolding in time order (12–20 s) · the human line (4 s, held) · what it means, one line (3 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** whose moment; the human line, verbatim; consent of everyone in frame.
- **Must be real:** footage or stills of that moment, or the person telling it.
- **Degrades to:** a text-and-voice story (the human line as a pull-quote text scene over the stills that exist). Without stills or the person, leave it out and rewrite.

### Explainer

- **Skeleton:** hook, the thing the viewer sees but does not understand (3 s) · signature (3 s) · what you see (6 s) · what is really happening, captions carrying the real names and numbers (12–18 s) · why it matters to them (5 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** which part; what the captions must leave the viewer knowing; whether a human sets the frame first (decision-maker cuts).
- **Must be real:** the part being explained, on screen at least once.
- **Degrades to:** a diagram or text scene for the mechanism is acceptable (one beat); the real thing still appears once. With nothing real, not buildable.

### Invitation

- **Skeleton:** hook, the viewer's hesitation or the morning (3 s) · signature (3 s) · what the first time looks like (10–15 s) · what you would actually do (6 s) · who you would be with (4 s) · CTA (4 s).
- **Brief must settle:** who is invited and to what, concretely; what they would do on their first time; never price.
- **Must be real:** people doing the thing, or the place.
- **Degrades to:** stills of the place and the people with a spoken or text line each. Without any, not buildable; a text-only invitation reads as an advert.

### Offer / pricing explainer

- **Skeleton (long):** the default, what is usually charged for (15 s) · the abstract (5 s) · how we do it (20 s) · tiers follow need, one by one (sequential, held) · what each does not include and why · "both ways cost something" · CTA.
- **Brief must settle:** exact contents from the live price page; the principle behind the pricing. Never a short; never a hook or CTA elsewhere.
- **Must be real:** the price page or the product itself at each tier.
- **Degrades to:** text scenes per tier over the product on screen are the natural form here; a voice is required.

## Inferring the type

`make-video` reads the concept's shape and proposes a type; it asks only when the concept reads as two:

| The concept… | Type |
|---|---|
| teaches one thing | how-to |
| sets two methods against each other | comparison |
| starts from a moment that cost something, and there is a fix to show | problem and fix |
| starts from a moment with a person in it, and the point is the moment | story |
| shows something new doing its job | reveal (footage) or launch (copy and one visual) |
| covers a whole job | walkthrough (and 3–5 shorts) |
| is about why the business exists | origin |
| makes a part of it understandable to an outsider | explainer |
| asks the viewer to turn up | invitation |
| is about what you pay | offer / pricing explainer |

## Signals that a concept is not ready

- The feature is on the roadmap and has not worked yet → brief it, mark it `on hold`, do not build.
- The claim is not in `facts.md` and has not been measured → get the fact first.
- The concept needs "and also" → split it.
- Nobody can say how the one viewer should feel at the end → the one idea is not clear yet.
- The hook is about the product → rewrite until it is about the viewer's moment.
- The type's must-be-real scenes are all gaps → the gap report says what the video becomes without them, and the person chooses.
