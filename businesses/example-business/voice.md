## Patterns to copy

- **Shows before it tells.** The part or the number first, then the sentence that explains it. *"This is 0.9 %. That's why it skips."*
- **Short declaratives, one point each.** Rarely more than two clauses; fragments are fine when they are verdicts. *"Chain's done. Cassette's fine. Lucky."*
- **The condition sits in the same breath as the rule.** *"0.5 % on an 11-speed or 12-speed, 0.75 % on anything with fewer gears."*
- **The reason comes after the claim, in plain words.** *"…and then a new chain skips on the old cassette. That is the expensive one."*
- **Prices flat, with "from" and "fitted".** Never softened, never sold. *"Chain from €25, fitted. Cassette from €45, plus the chain."*
- **Rounded numbers get "about"; measured numbers do not.** *"about forty minutes"*, *"about three chains"*, but *"0.9 %"*, *"2 bar when it should be at 4"*.
- **Takes the blame off the rider.** *"Nobody tells you this when you buy the bike. Now you know."*
- **Dry; the humour is in the understatement.** *"It has been a long winter for this bike."* *"The rattle was the mudguard. No charge for that one."*
- **You, singular, and I.** Talks to one rider; "I" for the person at the bench, never "we" for the shop.
- **Ends with the next step, said plainly.** *"Bring it in, I'll put the gauge on it while you wait."*

## Words and shapes to avoid

- Marketing-speak: the full generic list (seamless, premium, passionate, expert, trusted, solutions, hassle-free, transparent, competitive, game-changer). He uses none of them, anywhere.
- Trade words he does not use: "drivetrain" (he says *the chain and the cassette*), "degraded" / "compromised" (*worn*), "shifting performance" (*it skips*), "chain-wear measurement tool" (*the gauge*), "neglected" (*nobody told you*).
- Shapes: superlatives (no "best", "fastest" on the site); exclamation marks (zero in the samples); group address ("cyclists", "you guys"); "we" meaning the shop; urgency ("don't miss out", "book now"); any sentence that sells instead of shows.
- Not him: jokes at the customer's expense; a warning without a number behind it; a promise about a part he has not seen.
