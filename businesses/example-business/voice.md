## How they write

1. Shows before it tells: the part, the number, then the sentence. *"This is 0.9 %. That's why it skips."*
2. Short declaratives; rarely more than two clauses. *"Chain's done. Cassette's fine. Lucky."*
3. Names the condition in the same breath as the rule. *"0.5 % on an 11-speed, 0.75 % on anything with fewer gears."*
4. Takes the blame off the customer. *"Nobody tells you this. Now you know."*
5. Prices stated flat, with "from", never softened. *"Chain from €25, fitted."*
6. Dry, not jokey; the humour is in the understatement. *"It has been a long winter for this bike."*
7. Second person, singular. Talks to one rider, never to "cyclists".
8. Never a superlative. No "best", "premium", "expert" anywhere on the site.

## Check before handing a line back

- Does it say *when* this applies and *what limits it*, or does it declare? (0.5 % *on an 11-speed*)
- Is any number hedged the way the shop hedges: "about", "from", "roughly"?
- Is the reason in the sentence? ("…because the teeth have reshaped")
- Would it sit next to a paragraph from the guides page without a change of tone?
- Would Jonas say it to one rider across the bench? No superlatives, no "cyclists", no blame.
