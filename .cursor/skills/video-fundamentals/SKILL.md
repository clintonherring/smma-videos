# Video fundamentals

Read and follow `strategy/07-edit-fundamentals.md` (the edit), `strategy/06-delivery-and-narration.md` (how to speak), `strategy/11-text-scenes.md` (text as part of the video) and `strategy/10-assets-and-gaps.md` (what to do when a scene has no footage) together with the business's own files: `audience.md`, `voice.md`, `facts.md`, `brand.md`. The strategy owns the rules; this skill applies them to one video and says pass or fail.

## When planning or scripting

1. **Hook first.** Three hook options before the body (`strategy/02-hooks.md`). The first frame is footage of the moment, or the hook card when no footage exists and the gap decision says so. Never a title card, never a logo.
2. **Pick the signature.** The peer line for this viewer from `voice.md` (`strategy/03-signature.md`); the variant matches the audience track, never the wrong peer.
3. **Name the flow.** Write `entry → key action → result` for the centrepiece. If it cannot be written, the video is a brochure; rewrite until it can, or change the type.
4. **One type, one idea.** The type is settled at vet time (`strategy/08-video-types.md`) and fixes the skeleton. If the body needs "and also", it is two videos.
5. **Feeling.** The brief says how the one viewer should feel at the end, in a phrase. No feeling, no brief.
6. **Storyboard with durations and assets.** Every scene: on screen (asset ID or gap decision), spoken or narrated, caption that must be read, settled hold, flow beat, sequential or interaction moment, transition. Sum the durations; shorts land 20–60 s per the type's length, longs in chapters.
7. **Reading-time floor.** Labels ~0.8 s settled; sentences ~0.3 s per word, minimum ~1.2 s; the hook holds longest; a text scene carrying the idea gets +0.5 s. Fast in, then hold. Sequential readable text does not land every 0.5 s.
8. **Ground every claim.** Names, numbers, prices, capabilities and limits come from `facts.md` or were measured on the day; each carries its limit when the facts file gives one. Tone may invent; claims may not.
9. **Assets before words.** Every scene names the asset it plays or the gap decision (A placeholder, B text scene, C left out with the summary redone, D owner provides). A must-be-real scene for the type cannot take B without the brief saying what the video became.
10. **Text scenes carry one idea.** Brand colours and fonts, one line plus at most two small lines, at the floor, entrance from the type profile. It cannot be boring text; it also cannot be a paragraph.
11. **Out-loud test on every spoken line.** Would the speaker say this to one customer across the counter? If not, rewrite. A person talking, not a presenter.
12. **Voice check on every line** with `business-voice` mode 2: each line against `voice.md` "Patterns to copy" and "Words and shapes to avoid"; their phrases kept; numbers said the way they say numbers; no slogan; the signature as written there.
13. **One CTA**, from `channels.md` for the channel the video goes to first (`strategy/05-ctas-and-publishing.md`). Invitations do not sell; offers do not hook.

## When reviewing a brief, storyboard, take or cut

Run the pre-publish checklist in `strategy/07-edit-fundamentals.md` and the delivery checklist in `strategy/06-delivery-and-narration.md`. Fail it if any of these is true:

- The first 2 seconds are a title card, a logo, or a talking head on a plain background with nothing of the business in frame
- The centrepiece describes the product instead of showing the flow
- A caption or card the viewer must read is gone before the reading floor, or sequential text arrives faster than it can be read
- A claim, number, name or price is not in `facts.md` (or the day's measurements); a limit the facts file states was dropped
- A scene the type needs real (`08-video-types.md` "must be real") is a text scene without the brief saying the video degraded
- A gap has no decision, or a placeholder (A) is in a cut that is about to be published
- A text scene is off-brand, carries two ideas, or looks like a template; a placeholder looks finished
- A caption sits over the key action in a how-to, reveal or problem-and-fix
- Frame 0 is mid-transition, black, or an empty frame; the poster is not a settled moment
- A spoken line sounds like a presenter, a slogan, or someone else's brand; the signature is the wrong peer line
- Someone on camera does something they do not do in the real business, or is named without consent in `ASSETS.md`
- The video wants two ideas, two CTAs or two types
- No feeling was decided, or the cut does not land it
- Music, SFX or stock motion the owner did not supply

Pass or fail is said plainly, with the line, scene or frame that failed and the smallest change that fixes it. One list, not a lecture.

## Output shape when helping write a storyboard

```
### Scene N — [name] — [duration] s
On screen: [asset ID + in/out, or gap decision A/B/C/D with the card's one line]
Spoken / narrated:
Caption (must read):
Settled hold: [s] (floor: ok / short by [s])
Flow beat: entry / key action / result / frame / other
Sequential / interaction: [yes — what appears one by one or what action is shown] / none
Transition → next: [from the type profile]
```

Totals line at the end: scenes, duration, reads, text scenes, placeholders. `templates/storyboard.md` has the same block pre-filled per type.

## Where this sits

`make-video` loads this skill for the vet and the storyboard; `build-video` loads it before setting captions and text scenes and for the hand-back checklist; `video-schedule` does not need it. This skill does not start a video from an idea, write a brief unprompted, or build: backlog rows stay one-line ideas until the person picks one and approves the brief summary.
